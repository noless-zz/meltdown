## Summary

- `m3` gains a second step, `bot_seed_205_escape`. It flies the bot's recorded escape from
  generated seed 205.
- Suite steps take a new `seed` key. A step lists `level` or `seed`, never both, and either
  one needs a replay. With a seed, `AcceptanceRunner` builds the level with
  `SeedLevel.load_level`, the loader `--seed=` and the validator sweep use, from the shipped
  data files. It draws the level and flies the replay's inputs through
  `LocalSession.for_level`. The replay holds only inputs. A seed step whose replay carries
  walls, a reactor, robots or debris is refused.
- Two new checks:
  - `debris_fallen`: at least one debris entry is solid, or held back by the guard, at the end.
  - `level_hash`, with `expected_level_hash`: the hash of the session's replay header (walls,
    start, reactor, debris, tuning, weapons, robots, parts).
  - Why the level hash: the final hash only covers debris through which entries are solid.
    A change to a rock's radius left the flight's hash unchanged, so without it that change
    went unnoticed.
- New `acceptance/acceptance_step_level.gd` holds the seed set-up, the level hash and the
  replay-problem message that moved out of the runner. The runner is 295 lines (was 299).
- Why seed 205: over seeds 1..1000 with robots and debris, using `SeedLevel` levels, the bot
  escaped 69 seeds, but only 3 had debris down by the end: 205, 671 and 925.
  - 205 has the widest margin. The first rock falls at tick 4941, and the bot is out at 5093.
  - On 205 the bot destroys all 4 robots and finishes with 41.3 hull.
  - None of the escaping seeds with debris down has a loop. So no `generator.json` value
    changes seed 205. The generator-data test changes a module file instead.
