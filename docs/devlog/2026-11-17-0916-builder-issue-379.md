## Summary

- `EscapePath.known_route(walls, explored, from, to, debris, ship_radius)` returns a new
  `KnownRoute` (`game/core/known_route.gd`): `points_mm` (start, opening centres, exit, whole
  mm), `length_mm` (`UNREACHABLE` when none), `blocked_openings` and `blocked_centres_mm`.
- The search is `length_mm`'s Dijkstra on the explored boxes only: start, exit, explored box
  centres, then open openings between two explored boxes, lowest node on a tie, with
  predecessors kept. With every box explored and no debris the nodes are the same as
  `length_mm`'s, so the lengths agree. Box centres are left out of the points.
- Opening numbers count every pair of boxes i < j that share volume, explored or not, so a
  map mark keeps its number as the player explores. Blocked openings are reported only between
  two explored boxes.
- New `game/core/opening_clearance.gd` (`OpeningClearance`) decides "blocked". It takes the
  crossing axis as `LevelValidator.opening_widths` does (repeated, since core may not use
  gen). A ship crossing sweeps a cylinder of its radius through the opening's depth, so each
  solid sphere becomes a disc in the cross-section, using its nearest point of that depth. The
  opening is blocked when those discs cover the rectangle shrunk by the ship's radius. Only
  rectangle corners, disc–side and disc–disc meeting points are tested, in 64-bit floats
  (Godot's vectors are single precision). An opening narrower than a ship without debris is
  not called blocked.
- `EscapePath._to_mm` and `_edge` became the public `to_mm` and `edge_mm`. Nothing in `Sim`,
  `longest_for_ships` or the meltdown timer changed, so no pinned hash moved.

## Open questions

- Spheres are round, so a ship can pass above or below one that narrows an opening at its
  equator. The tests' numbers follow that, rather than the narrowest gap at one height.
- Debris inside a box (not across an opening) is not considered; the route still runs
  through the box.
