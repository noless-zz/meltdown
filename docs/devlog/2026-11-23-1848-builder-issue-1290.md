## Issue

#1290: sound cues. Core events play positional sounds from a data table (AUD-1, PLT-1,
ARC-1, ARC-4, NAM-3).

## Summary

- `data/audio/cues.json`: `max_voices`, `quiet_db`, one row per cue (sound, `volume_db`,
  `pitch_min`, `pitch_max`, optional `min_magnitude`) and a `silent` list. Every
  `SimEvent.Kind` is a cue or silent; silent are the afterburner start and stop, energy
  depleted, salvage dropped, lost in meltdown (ship destroyed follows it) and debris held.
- Shots and bolts hitting walls are not `SimEvent`s, so `SoundCueEvents` reads them from
  the bolts: a bolt seen first is `SHOT_FIRED:<weapon id>` from the slot that fired it
  (`SHOT_FIRED:robot` for robots, plain `SHOT_FIRED` for a weapon with no row); a bolt gone
  with range left places the ROBOT_HIT or SHIP_HIT it matches by owner and direction, and
  otherwise plays `BOLT_IMPACT`.
- Places: events with a position use it; a robot hit and kill sound where the bolt ended;
  a ship hit 2 m back along the shot; a wall knock 1 m into the wall. WALL_IMPACT needs
  0.5 m/s (`min_magnitude`); sliding along a wall reports about 0.1 m/s every tick (1019
  impacts over `bot_g2_escape`).
- `SoundCues` (view): pooled `AudioStreamPlayer3D`s under the world node; the current
  camera (the XR camera) is the listener, so cues turn with the head. Disabled under the
  dummy driver. `SoundVoices` caps voices; past the cap the oldest quiet voice (at or
  below `quiet_db`) goes first, else the oldest.
- `CombatWiring.attach` adds the player; `update` ends with `feed_sound`, which forgets
  the bolts when the simulation changes (new level).
- 19 placeholder sounds synthesised by `tools/synth_sounds.py` (stdlib, seeded,
  reproducible); recorded in `docs/licences.md`.
- Acceptance: `suites/m6/sound.json` step `sound_cues` on the existing
  `both_primaries_fire.replay` (hash 1302297426, unchanged). The check
  `sound_cues_in_order` lives in `acceptance/checks/sound_cues_check.gd`, with its
  expected cues by step id in `acceptance/checks/sound_cues.json`; it re-flies the replay,
  checks the cues in order and that the hash with the cues followed is the pinned one.
