## Summary

- New `game/view/robot_skin.gdshader`: the robot body's material. A procedural pattern (rings,
  chevrons, checks, bands, stripes, grid or dots) in an accent colour, worked out from the position
  in the robot's own frame (each part passes its size and offset as instance uniforms), so it is
  continuous across body and parts and turns with the robot. A face plate covers everything more
  than `face_depth` ahead along the robot's forward (-z), so the eye sits on a dark visor. Lines are
  softened by `fwidth` so far patterns do not shimmer. No textures, no imported art.
- #604's effects are applied after the pattern and face: `brightness` (damage dim) scales the
  surface, `flash` mixes the whole surface to the flash colour, `energy` (hit and wind-up glow)
  scales the emission.
- New `game/view/robot_skin.gd` (`RobotSkin`): parses the optional `skin` object of a look entry
  (`pattern`, `accent_color`, `scale`, `width`, `face_color`, `face_depth`), sets the shader's
  uniforms, and mirrors the face rule for tests.
- `RobotLook` accepts `skin` (no skin: plain). `RobotView` uses a `ShaderMaterial` body instead of a
  `StandardMaterial3D`; `body_material()` now returns it. Eyes are unchanged.
- `robot_look.json`: a skin per type. Drone rings, glider chevrons, hauler checks, needler bands,
  skimmer stripes, warden grid; each with its own dark accent and a dark face plate.
