## Summary

- New look file `xr/hub_screen.json`, parsed by `HubScreenLook` (every key checked, the
  bad key named, a built-in fallback equal to the file). It holds the screen and word
  colours, font sizes, line heights, rows shown (5), the focus movement and the
  thresholds: contrast 7:1, 0.9 degrees and 15 px of capital height, screen emission 0.35,
  and the Quest 3 eye buffer (2064 x 2208, 96 degrees vertical).
- `HubStationScreen` now draws its own dark, unshaded face (so the contrast seen is the
  contrast in the file, whatever the room's lights do) with light words on it. A frame of
  four bars goes round the station A would use and the one in use. The station in use
  comes 0.45 m towards the eye and grows 1.8 times over 0.4 s, eased; leaving sends it
  back. Only the screen moves; the room and camera do not (CMF-8). Rows too wide for the
  face wrap onto a second line, and the window then holds fewer rows, always with the
  selected one.
- `HubScreenMeasure`: WCAG contrast, capital height, angle and pixel arithmetic, and a
  walk over the room's visible labels; shared by the tests and the check.
- `data/hub/room.json`: screen material emission 0.9 → 0.25 (launch 1.0 → 0.3), so no
  screen outshines the engine glow. `view/hub_room_parts.gd`: the station name labels
  above the screens go from font 40 to 56, to pass the same 0.9 degree floor.
- Acceptance: new `hub_screens` check and step in `suites/m6/base.json`. It opens the
  room after a lost run, then reads it while choosing and with each of the six stations
  in use and settled. It reports the smallest text in pixels and degrees and the lowest
  contrast, and fails under the thresholds, on a word wider than its face, on a screen
  that glows too much, or on an unframed choice. Sizes are projected from the scene's
  nodes, not read from a rendered image (the headless runner draws nothing).
