## Summary

- `view/ship_look.json` and `ShipLook`: named physically based materials (hull, trim,
  accent, canopy), an engine glow (colour, idle and full energy) and up to 12 primitive
  pieces with shape, size, offset, turn and material. Every bad field is named; a refused
  or missing file keeps a plain capsule hull with an engine glow and warns.
- `view/ship_model.gd` (`ShipModel`, Node3D): `show_at(transform, thrust)` and
  `set_thrust` are its whole input, so it reads and writes no simulation object (MP-2).
  Lit materials are shared per look; each ship has its own engine material so its thrust
  shows on its own. Adds no light (GFX-4). `cost()` reports pieces, draw calls, materials
  and triangles.
- The design (NAM-3, original): a pale fuselage with a dark glass canopy forward, a
  trim nose cone and chin gun, a swept delta wing, two raked orange wingtip fins and one
  rear engine with an orange ring around a blue glow. Rendered locally from the front,
  side, three-quarter and top under xvfb: it reads as a ship with a clear nose.
- Draw cost: 10 pieces, 10 draw calls, 5 materials (4 shared lit + 1 engine per ship),
  960 triangles. Target was at most 12 draw calls (PRF-4).
- Acceptance: step `ship_model` in `suites/m6/ship.json` (no replay). The new
  `ship_model` check fails a refused look, more than 12 draw calls, any light, or an
  engine that does not brighten with thrust; it then hangs the ship 4 m before the scene's
  camera nose-on and side-on and saves `m6/ship_model_front.png` and `_side.png`.
