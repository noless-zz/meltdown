## Summary

- A `MeltdownDebris` entry may set `"kind": "fire"` (default `"solid"`) with
  `damage_per_second`, held as whole thousandths per second in `fire_milli_dps` (0 for
  solid; a short array means solid, so `DebrisPlan` needs no change).
- `is_solid` now means "fallen and not fire"; the new `has_fallen` and `is_burning` cover
  the rest. Every blocker (`contain_impact`, `segment_entry`, `OpeningClearance`,
  `LevelRoute`, the bot's re-plan) already asks `is_solid`, so fire is passable everywhere
  without touching those files.
- `DebrisRouteGuard` skips fire entries when they fall due and leaves them out of its
  "already fallen" list, so it never holds fire back.
- New `core/meltdown_fire.gd` (`MeltdownFire`): reads and writes the two keys and burns.
  `ShipContact.apply` calls `MeltdownFire.burn` after the walls, so each live ship whose
  centre is inside a burning entry (surface included) takes a sixtieth of its damage per
  second through `ShipState.apply_damage` each tick. `sim.gd` is untouched (295 lines).
- `to_json` writes `kind` and `damage_per_second` only for fire, after `after_seconds`.
  Fire data is fixed for the run and not hashed; damage shows in the ship's hash.
- `meltdown_debris.gd` is 288 lines.
